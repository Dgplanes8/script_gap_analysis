Go-to-Market Strategy: AI-Powered User Acquisition for Digital Subscription Services
1. Executive Summary
This document outlines a go-to-market strategy for a specialized service leveraging a rapid, AI-powered workflow to develop high-performance creative concepts and audience targeting for digital subscription businesses. The primary goal is to solve the critical challenge of sustainable user acquisition for this sector. By combining a decade of top-tier performance marketing experience with this efficient AI process, the service is positioned to deliver immediate, tangible value. The target is to achieve $1k MRR within 60 days and scale to $10k MRR within 180 days.

2. The Winning Niche: Digital Subscription Services
This is the ideal market segment. These companies have recurring revenue models, which means they have a predictable LTV and a constant, urgent need for new user acquisition to combat churn and drive growth. Your ESPN+ background gives you instant credibility and a deep understanding of their operating model.

Sub-Niches to Target First:

Niche SaaS: Companies with an ACV (Annual Contract Value) between $500 - $5,000. They have a marketing budget but often lack a sophisticated in-house creative strategy team.

Paid Newsletters & Communities: The creator economy is booming. High-growth newsletters (e.g., on Substack Pro, Beehiiv, Ghost) need to constantly find new subscribers outside of their organic channels.

Specialty/Niche Streaming Services: Think Shudder (horror), CuriosityStream (documentaries), or MUBI (indie film). They compete with giants and need highly efficient, targeted campaigns to win.

Consumer & Prosumer Apps: Focus on non-gaming apps in categories like productivity, fitness, education, or finance that rely on a subscription model (e.g., Strava, Duolingo, Headspace).

3. Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
Your target buyer is not the CMO of a Fortune 500 company. It's the person on the front lines of growth who is resource-constrained and under pressure to deliver results.

Title: Founder, Head of Growth, Head of Marketing, or Senior Performance Marketing Manager.

Company Size: 5-75 employees. Large enough to have a dedicated marketing budget, but small enough that senior people are still "in the weeds."

Key Characteristics:

Manages a monthly ad budget of at least $10k-$50k+.

Is highly data-driven and measured on acquisition KPIs (CAC, LTV, trial-to-paid conversion rate).

Is likely wearing multiple hats and is time-poor.

Understands the value of creative but struggles to produce it at the speed and scale required for performance testing.

4. Customer Pain Points & Your Solution
Pain Point

Your 10-minute workflow delivers multiple, data-informed creative briefs and concepts, ready for design. You eliminate the bottleneck.

6. Monetization & Pricing Models
Start with a service model to validate the offering and build case studies. Evolve to a productized service or SaaS later.

Model 1: The "Concept Sprint" (One-Off Project)

Offer: A comprehensive analysis of their business, delivering 3-5 unique creative concepts (with full briefs, hooks, and body copy) and 2 new target audience profiles.

Price: $1,500 - $2,500. This is a low-risk entry point for clients. One or two of these hits your $1k MRR goal immediately.

Model 2: The "Growth Partner" (Subscription Service)

Offer: A monthly retainer for a set number of "Creative Drops." For example, 2 drops per month, with each drop containing 2-3 new concepts and 1 new audience.

Price: $2,000 - $4,000 / month. This is your path to $10k MRR. It creates predictable revenue and integrates you into their growth process.
